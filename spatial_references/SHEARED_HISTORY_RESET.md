# Can a two-observation reset recover when a tilted wall moves?

Pooling the sparse offset scores across rounds restored all 64 stationary tilted offset vectors and nearly matched supplied geometry. Accumulation can also keep old evidence after the wall moves. Use eight fresh 192³ worlds, the same frozen local detector, the same sparse observations and a single independent one-axis offset jump at the start of round nine. The tilt stays fixed within each world. The aligned companion stays stationary. [Stationary comparison](sheared_history_study/README.md)

Compare current-frame offset estimates, indefinite pooling and pooling with a per-axis reset. Reset an axis when the current and immediately prior valid observations agree within four periodic cells and are both at least twelve cells from the previously accumulated offset. Replace that axis's accumulated evidence by the two confirming profiles. Both pooled arms restart when the estimated slope changes. The selected slope, 64 lines per axis, 1,024 witness anchors per region, cell dynamics and write streams are matched. No arm receives the true offsets or the jump schedule.

Score exact offset vectors and retained words over rounds 10–12, allowing one observation for confirmation. Require at least 85% complete offset recovery and 20 points above indefinite pooling, a one-point retention gain with positive paired lower bound, a lower bound at least −0.5 points versus current-frame routing, no more than half a point of pre-jump loss at either lower bound, and at most four false resets across the aligned companion. Resample whole worlds. Report detection and retention separately. This benchmark covers one prespecified discrete movement family and a frozen checkpoint. [Runner](sheared_history_reset.py)
