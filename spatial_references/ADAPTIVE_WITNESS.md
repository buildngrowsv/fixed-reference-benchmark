# Can a write gate choose a new confirmation direction when correlated faults rotate?

The side-64 witness experiment showed that separation helped against compact faults and lost its advantage when each fault reached across the whole separation axis. Give the same 262,144-cell grid a choice of three equal-distance witness locations, eight cells along each coordinate axis. Use the original three trained local detectors and 32-bit transport controllers. Each gate can consult one spatial witness per write. A shared controller can observe the prior binary primary-gate field from the frozen detector. It cannot observe the write schedule, intended words, fault masks or future fields.

Each of 12 worlds includes two paired variants with 256 transitions. One fault orientation stays fixed. The other rotates to one of the other two axes after round eight. Each fault draws aligned 4×4 columns covering exactly a quarter of the grid. The two variants share write locations, movement, physical fault draws before rotation, and the one-percent dropout of legitimate witness signals. Compare four policies. A fixed axis is balanced across worlds, a calibration-only policy minimizes co-activation during the first four rounds, an adaptive policy keeps minimizing an exponential average of co-activation with decay .92, and a geometry oracle knows which axis the columns run along. The observed statistic is the fraction of primary detections simultaneously active at both endpoints, averaged per candidate direction. Both learned choices use observations through the previous tick. The oracle is an information-rich control. All four arms use the same two-tick primary confirmation and one spatial witness.

Score exact word recovery after the fault interval and the fraction of authorized and false writes accepted. Report the last eight rounds and choices made on the last false pulse of each round, holding all three checkpoints together in a bootstrap over worlds. On rotated faults require at least five points of improvement over the calibration-only and balanced fixed baselines with positive lower paired bounds, acceptance of at least 98% of legitimate writes, and at most a two-point loss against calibration-only under stable geometry. This tests whether the extra shared sensor statistic can adapt spatial routing on a known candidate set. Report the runtime of the whole comparison without attributing it to a single policy.
