# Do local witness choices help when fault directions disagree across a volume?

The global adaptive router changed confirmation direction when the same fault orientation rotated across its whole grid. Test whether restricting the estimator to eight local regions helps under simultaneous conflicting directions. Run eight independent 96³ grids for 256 transitions with each of the three frozen movement and write checkpoints. Each 48³ region exposes the same three candidate witnesses at distance eight, with periodic wrap within that region. The same boundary and one-witness budget apply to every arm.

Draw independent 4×4 columns in every region at exactly 25% fault coverage. Pair a homogeneous world whose regions share one orientation with a heterogeneous world whose eight axes contain three of one direction, three of another and two of the third, with unseen ordering. Keep each region's underlying fault draw, legitimate signals, noise, and exact movement schedule paired between worlds. Keep orientation constant during each world. Compare global co-activation, the same statistic and decay computed separately in each region, and a fixed perpendicular decision informed by the actual orientation per region. The local router does not receive neighboring detector fields. Its candidate wires and their boundary remain supplied.

Score exact 32-bit recovery after faults, true and false write acceptance, and how often a route aligns with the fault direction. Aggregate over the last eight rounds. Resample worlds holding all checkpoint seeds together. Require at least five percentage points advantage with positive paired lower bound in the heterogeneous case, at most two points loss in the homogeneous case, and at least 98% legitimate writes accepted. Record allocation and elapsed time. The homogeneous comparison tests a regime where a shared statistic has the right spatial support.
