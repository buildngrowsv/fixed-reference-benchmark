# Do 64 probes per plane still work on a 7-million-cell grid?

On the 96³ grid, 64 stratified probes per plane met the two-point retention margin against full observation. Their equal-budget advantage over random placement missed the superiority gate. This experiment tests the stronger scale claim. Keep the probe count at 64 and run the algorithm on a 192³ grid with 7,077,888 cells. The grid has eight times the volume. The three-axis field fraction used for boundary estimation falls from at most 2.08% to at most 0.521%.

Freeze the three recorded detectors and transport rules. Keep the regional witness distance at eight cells, the four-cell fault tiles, the eight-region periodic split family, the local update and the decision rules. Each plane is divided into an 8×8 grid of 24×24 tiles. The known translation ranges over the larger set of shifts allowed by the same generator. Generate eight new worlds with indices 32 through 39. Pair full-field and stratified observation within each world with identical initial words, commands, fault geometry and true writes. The fixed and known-boundary outcomes must agree exactly. The report gives exact translated coordinates, 32-bit memory retention, false writes and accepted legitimate writes for those paired worlds.

Require the 95% paired lower bound against full observation under shifted faults to stay above −2 points, at least a four-point retention gain over fixed regions with a positive paired lower bound, and at least 98% legitimate writes under both fault layouts. Resample entire worlds and keep checkpoints and measured rounds together. A successful result would support keeping the absolute number of detector locations fixed across this scale jump in the given grid and fault family.
