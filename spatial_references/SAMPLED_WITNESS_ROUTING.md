# Can the same detector probes choose the witness direction?

The 192³ benchmark used at most 0.521% of the field to locate boundaries and then read the full detector field to select the witness axis inside each region. This comparison asks whether the calculations that select each witness can also use the sampled lines. [192³ retention result](probe_scale_study/README.md)

Keep the 64 stratified lines per axis from the 96³ comparison, including their placements and all eight world seeds. The inferred boundaries must match the published array exactly. For each inferred region and each candidate axis, count pairs of active readings eight cells apart along the probe lines. Both cells are on the same line, and the displacement wraps inside the region. Divide by the number of sampled pairs in that region and choose the axis with the lowest rate. Use the same frozen learned detectors, transport, witnesses, three checkpoints, stimuli and updates. Keep the fixed and supplied-boundary controls unchanged and verify their exact released counts. Change only how the inferred arm estimates regional co-activation.

The paired conditions are the previous stratified boundary inference with dense witness routing and the new stratified boundary inference with sampled witness routing. On both aligned and shifted faults, require the paired 95% lower retention bound to reach −1.0 point versus the dense witness policy. Also require a shifted-fault gain of at least four points over fixed geometry with a positive lower bound, and at least 98% legitimate writes in both layouts. Bootstrap the same complete world seeds. The inferred policy uses at most 2.08% of the field for the two decision statistics together. This is an information-budget experiment. The paired simulator still stages the full detector field and keeps the dense comparator available. No device-transfer or wall-clock improvement forms part of the decision.
