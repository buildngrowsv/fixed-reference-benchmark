# Can dedicated detector pairs recover regional routing at less than 6% sampling?

Reusing the boundary lines to choose witness directions reduced shifted-fault retention by 3.02 points on the 96³ grid even with exactly matched boundary estimates. This test allocates detector samples directly to the witness decision. The aim is to recover the dense orientation policy under a still-small observation budget.

Keep the previous 64 stratified boundary lines per axis, three frozen checkpoints and eight paired worlds. In each inferred region choose 1,024 uniformly sampled base coordinates with replacement. Fix them in regional coordinates for each world. Read each base and its three eight-cell-offset neighbors, with wrap within the region. Compute all three co-activation scores on the same base sample, then choose the lowest. This adds at most 32,768 scalar readings per layout to the boundary-line observations, for a combined upper bound of 5.79% of the detector field. The stressor, witness distance, local learned operations and exact readout stay fixed. The larger sample budget and point arrangement change together in this practical test.

Compare with the published dense witness scores and with the failed policy that reused the boundary lines. The stored boundaries and the fixed and known controls must match both archives exactly. Require the paired lower retention bound versus dense routing to reach −1 point in both layouts, a shifted gain of at least two points over reused lines with a positive lower bound, at least four points over fixed geometry with a positive lower bound, and at least 98% legitimate writes. This tests the information available to the orientation statistic. The simulator still stages dense detector data for its controls.
