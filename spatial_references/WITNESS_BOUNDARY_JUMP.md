# Can the sparse estimator recover after the regional boundaries jump?

The prior 192³ experiment used the same hidden translation throughout a world. Use that frozen detector, transport, 64 stratified lines per axis and 1,024 dedicated anchor points per region. Translate all three boundary offsets by another 48 cells at the ninth round, using periodic wrap within 96 cells. The aligned companion keeps its original geometry. Keep the eight original world seeds and the first frozen checkpoint. Hold the calibrated control at its true first-half boundaries for the whole run. A true-boundary control receives the jump at the same round as the sampled estimator.

Require exact equality between the calibrated and true-boundary controls before the jump. After the jump, require the sampled recovery's paired lower bound versus the true-boundary control to be at least −2 points, a gain of at least four points over the stale calibrated control with a positive lower bound, and at least 98% legitimate-write acceptance in both layouts. Resample complete worlds. Report measurements by epoch and the inferred offsets. This tests an abrupt in-world change, with the same under-1% detector budget and sample coordinates. The study makes no end-to-end speed comparison. [Static-boundary replication](anchored_witness_scale_study/README.md)
