# How much of the apparent jump response came from earlier saved words?

After staggered jumps, the sparse observer's immediate retention margin over stale calibration was +1.47 points pooled and +4.94 at the third jump. By the third jump, the policies had made different writes over several rounds. Their starting banks differed, so the per-round score mixes acute response and history. A matched intervention can hold the starting bank fixed. [First-round staggered evidence](witness_staggered_jumps_study/README.md)

Replay the same eight world seeds, 192³ grid, 24 scheduled jumps, learned weights and observation policy. Before each jump, copy the supplied-boundary arm's saved 32-bit word bank into the other arms. Keep the stale geometry, detector observations, movement streams, write streams and boundary schedules fixed. Score the end of the marked round under all three policies. Between events, let histories evolve normally. This design holds the initial state at each jump to the supplied-boundary trajectory. It does not average over other possible shared starting states.

The supplied-boundary arm must reproduce its original tallies exactly. Across both schedules and all policies, the counts for exposure, write acceptance and false writes, and all detected and actual offsets, must match the archive exactly. These checks separate a change in saved-word history from any change in the current routing or detection. Archive raw cell totals and compare the original sampled-minus-stale retention gain to the common-start gain on the same 24 events. Bootstrap whole worlds in 5,000 paired draws. Require a gain change of at least one percentage point in absolute value and a 95% interval excluding zero to attribute a material difference to saved-word history. If the interval lies within ±1 point, the gain is robust to this common start. Report unresolved otherwise. [Implementation](witness_common_start.py)
