# E3 protocol: walls from scratch

Registered 20 November 2026, before any full run. Code: `e3/` in this repository. Program plan: [README](../README.md#e3-walls-from-scratch-a-network-you-can-operate-on).

## Question

If a network is trained with walls, do its skills end up in places you can remove, swap or add to with little collateral damage? And what does that cost in quality compared with a dense network of the same size and training?

## Data and tokenizer

- **Training mixture:** English (FineWeb-Edu sample-10BT), Python (codeparrot-clean files 1 and 2) and Welsh (FineWeb-2 cym_Latn). Every batch holds 40 sequences of 512 tokens from each domain, in an order fixed by the seed.
- **Held back for growth:** Icelandic (FineWeb-2 isl_Latn). No main run sees it.
- **Validation:** 64 fixed windows per domain from the published validation files. For English, the validation set is every hundredth document.
- **Tokenizer:** byte-level BPE with 8,192 entries, trained on an equal-character mix of the three training domains (60M characters each). The `prog-e3-data` job records its SHA-256. Compression is 3.6 bytes per token for English, 3.1 for Python, 3.4 for Welsh and 1.8 for Icelandic.

## Arms

All arms are pre-norm decoders with RoPE, SwiGLU feed-forward layers and untied embeddings. They have roughly equal block parameters and are trained on identical tokens.

| Arm | Residual stream | What each module may touch | Parameters (screen / main) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dense | d = 384 / 768 | everything | 16.9M / 97.6M |
| Walled | 4 rooms of d/2, plus 16 / 32 shared door channels | its own room and the doors | 24.3M / 117.7M |
| Topographic | d | everything, with a wiring-length penalty | 16.9M / 97.6M |
| Routed | d | dense attention; 4 feed-forward room experts of half the dense hidden size, top-2 router | 24.0M / 154.2M (active equal to dense) |

- **Walled.** Rooms are a batched dimension of the weights, so walls hold by construction rather than by masking. The embedding writes every channel and the unembedding reads every channel. Four rooms of width d/2 have the same block parameters as one stream of width d (4 · (d/2)² = d²). The walled model's extra parameters are the wider embeddings and the doors.
- **Topographic.** Every residual channel, attention dimension and hidden unit has a fixed position on a circle. After every update, each block weight shrinks toward zero by learning rate × λ × the circular distance between its two ends. This is a proximal step for the penalty λ · Σ |W| · distance, in the spirit of brain-inspired modular training. The screen tests λ = 0.01 and 0.05.
- **Routed.** A learned router sends each token to two of four experts. A Switch-style balance loss with weight 0.01 keeps the experts in use.

## Runs

| Job | What | Tokens | Estimated hours |
|---|---|---|---:|
| `prog-e3-data` | tokenizer and shards | 150M per domain, 40M Icelandic | 0.3 |
| `prog-e3-screen` | 5 screen runs: dense, walled, topographic ×2, routed | 200M each | 3.5 |
| `prog-e3-dense` | main dense | 400M | 2.5 |
| `prog-e3-walled-s1` | main walled, seed 1 | 400M | 5 |
| `prog-e3-walled-s2` | main walled, seed 2 (the transplant donor) | 400M | 5 |
| `prog-e3-topographic` | main topographic at the screened λ | 400M | 2.5 |
| `prog-e3-routed` | main routed | 400M | 3 |
| `prog-e3-surgery` | removal summary, transplant, growth | about 55M | 2 |

The token counts are set by measured throughput. The main walled model trains at about 21,000 tokens per second, against 45,000 for dense. Training uses AdamW with peak learning rates of 1.5e-3 (screen) and 6e-4 (main), 2% warmup and a cosine decay to 10%. Weight decay is 0.1.

## Measurements

- **Quality cost of walls:** mean validation loss over the three training domains, walled against dense.
- **Room removal:** remove one room so that its modules write nothing, then measure each domain's loss increase. The rooms are:
  - the walled model's rooms;
  - the routed model's experts;
  - for the dense and topographic models, four contiguous arcs of attention dimensions and hidden units. The dense arcs are the no-structure reference.
- **Selectivity index:** S = (largest increase − mean of the other two) / (largest + mean of the other two). S ≥ 0.5 means the largest domain increase is at least three times the others. Loss decreases count as zero. A room counts only if its removal raises its top domain's loss by at least 1%.
- **Transplant:** the most selective room of walled seed 1 is replaced by the same room from walled seed 2. That includes its block weights and its slices of the embedding, unembedding and final norm. Then 400 updates refit only the door weights. The control removes the room outright and gets the same door-only refit. Recovery = (loss with the room removed − loss after transplant and refit) / (the removal's damage), measured on the room's domain.
- **Growth:** a fifth room is added. It is initialised so the model computes exactly the same function. Three arms train for 500 updates on Icelandic only:
  - the new room plus all door weights;
  - the new room alone (sealed);
  - full fine-tuning of the walled model (the forgetting reference).

  Reported: Icelandic gain and the change on each old domain.

## Success and stopping

- **Success (`walls_success`):** walls cost at most 3% in mean loss, and every training domain has a walled room with S ≥ 0.5. The transplant also recovers at least 90% of the removal damage and beats the door-refit control by at least 25 points of recovery.
- **Partial (`walls_partial`):** some room has S ≥ 0.5, or the transplant works, but not every criterion holds.
- **Null (`walls_null`):** none of the above.
- **Stop rule:** if the screen shows S ≤ 0.5 for every room of both the walled and topographic arms, `prog-e3-screen` returns `screen_no_selectivity`. The main runs still go, because the screen is small, but the paper reports that the stop rule fired.

## Prior work

- Brain-inspired modular training (Liu, Gan and Tegmark, 2023).
- TopoLM (2024).
- Branch-Train-Merge (Li et al., 2022) and Branch-Train-MiX (Sukhbaatar et al., 2024).
- Mixture-of-experts language models.

Our contribution is a head-to-head comparison at equal size and data of hard walls, a wiring penalty and routing, measured by what surgery each one allows.
