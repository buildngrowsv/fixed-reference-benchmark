# Can the wide tunnel encode the identifier at its source, and does the rest of the model use it?

Swapping the fixed 192 channels just before the final block broke 2 of 935 previously solved identifier pairs. Swapping the neighboring 192 broke 113. Neither partial swap reversed both answers. This leaves open whether the protected slice lacks the distinguishing letter, contains it but is rarely read, or can affect the prediction only if the edit happens earlier. Test this on the existing 285M checkpoint. No transformer weights change. [Earlier swap](large_study/held_out/README.md)

Use 1,024 calibration pairs at generator indices 16000–17023 and 512 fresh evaluation pairs at 18000–18511. Fit one ridge readout per slice to predict the letter at the declaration site from block-2 activations. Both readouts have 192 inputs and 26 outputs and use the same calibration pairs. Report both-member success in a two-choice comparison on the fresh pairs. This measures whether the slice retains an accessible signal. For the causal test, exchange only the designated or neighboring 192 coordinates at the single differing source byte as block 2 exits. Run all eight subsequent blocks with their original settings. A whole-state exchange at the source byte is a localization control. Swapping every position at block 2 is a positive control and must reproduce the partner’s final scores.

A specialist reference effect requires at least half of fresh pairs to be solved initially, a five-point advantage for fixed over neighboring swaps in exact two-member reversals over all pairs, and a positive lower 95% paired bound. For the narrower hypothesis of a readable but weakly used representation, report whether the fixed readout solves at least 80% while the whole-site exchange reverses at least 20% and the fixed-slice exchange reverses fewer than 5%. The readouts receive letters from a synthetic task. A successful readout demonstrates decodability, and causal usage requires the separately reported interventions. The earlier wide checkpoint exchange took nine seconds on the Studio. The longer scoring and calibration here should finish within two minutes.
