Stable displaced addressing vastly outperformed uniformly redrawn locations of identical density. Localize how the trained, displaced head uses that address. Take 1,024 fresh paired programs. In each, change only the four-token initialization event's value byte for the register queried at the end. Force a subsequent write to this register at event 40. Score the original-value question, with distinct correct values in the two members. The initializer is equally likely to appear anywhere in the initial 16 events.

Freeze both trained checkpoints. In the displaced model, swap just the first head's 64-dimensional value vector between paired programs at the endpoint of the event one past initialization. Apply its effect only to the final query in block 4. Attention keys, queries, allowed edges, other heads and every other query are fixed. A matched control exchanges the value at the equally allowed shifted write, event 41. Perform corresponding exchanges at the unshifted initialization and write in the trained correct-address model. Verify both positions are visible and that self exchange reproduces the unmodified logits exactly. Report pairwise accuracy and how often both predictions switch to the other program's correct value. Count the causal switches only among pairs originally correct on both members. Support specific carriage of the original value in the displaced head if the source swap produces at least 25 percentage points more double switches than the later-write exchange over all pairs, with a positive paired 95% bootstrap lower bound. A high disruption rate alone does not establish transport of the specific alternative value. This observes a causal contribution at one head in trained synthetic models. It cannot establish a unique route through preceding layers.
