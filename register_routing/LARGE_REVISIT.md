# Does a larger dense model catch up to the engineered register read?

On the original 14.3M-parameter, eight-block model, one displaced-address head achieved 92.26% balanced register recall while dense attention reached 8.85% at 512 updates. Repeat the same two arms at width 1,536, four times the original width and approximately sixteen times as many weights. Keep eight blocks, 64 numbers per head, the same single addressed head in block 4, and the same initialization procedure, examples, optimizer, batch of 32 and 512 updates. The addressed head occupies one of 24 heads at the larger width and one of six at the smaller width. This matches examples and updates. The larger networks consume more compute.

Report balanced original/current recall on the identical 1,024 held-out programs, the displaced-minus-dense gap at each width, and the difference of these gaps with a paired 95% program-bootstrap interval. A narrowing exceeding ten percentage points with an interval excluding zero counts as a material interaction. A large-model gap of at least ten points with a positive lower bound supports a continued routing benefit. Both outcomes can coexist. Report all four scores. Reuse archived small-model counts after checking program denominators and heuristic predictions.

Repeat the original 1,024-pair value exchange on the wider displaced model. Count pairs where both correct original-value answers switch to their specific partner values after one 64-value activation is exchanged at the displaced initialization site. The matched control swaps the later write. One coupled training seed and a fixed synthetic task bound these conclusions. A null at this budget does not establish what a converged model could learn.
